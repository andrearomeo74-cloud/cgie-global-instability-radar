# Congruity Glossary v1.0

Status:

DRAFT

---

# Purpose

This glossary defines the canonical terminology used throughout
the Congruity Framework.

Every implementation, experiment, report and publication should
use these definitions consistently.

When ambiguity exists, the glossary prevails over implementation
documents.

---

# Identity

The operational description of what makes a declared system remain
the same functional system through time.

Identity is not equivalent to the observed state.

---

# Functional Identity

The minimum relational organization required for a declared system
to continue performing its declared function.

---

# Candidate Identity

A proposed operational representation of functional identity that
remains subject to experimental validation.

---

# Identity Network

The graph composed of eligible relations representing the current
candidate identity.

---

# Relation

An observable connection between two or more system components.

Relations may be statistical, temporal, spatial, structural,
functional or domain-specific.

---

# Candidate Relation

A measurable relation that may contribute to the identity network.

---

# Eligible Relation

A candidate relation satisfying all frozen experimental criteria.

---

# Primary Relation

An eligible relation selected by the deterministic protocol as
part of the candidate identity network.

Primary does not imply indispensable.

---

# Secondary Relation

An eligible relation retained for redundancy or sensitivity
analysis.

---

# Non-identifiable Relation

A relation that cannot be classified reliably using the available
evidence.

---

# Identity Continuity

The degree to which the candidate identity remains compatible with
its frozen baseline representation.

---

# Structural Continuity

Preservation of the structural organization defined by the
declared implementation.

---

# Congruity

The degree of proportional consistency between the declared
function of a system and the organization that supports it.

Congruity is always context dependent.

---

# Functional Boundary

The declared limits within which the identity of a system is
evaluated.

---

# Context

The collection of external conditions under which observations are
interpreted.

---

# Observation

A measured representation of some aspect of the declared system.

Observations are not the system itself.

---

# Projection

An observable manifestation of the underlying system.

Observed variables are treated as projections rather than identity
itself.

---

# Transition

A measurable change in relational organization.

A transition is not necessarily a collapse.

---

# Collapse

Loss of the declared functional identity beyond the admissible
boundary.

---

# Recovery

Partial or complete restoration of functional identity after
degradation.

---

# Admissibility

The degree to which an interpretation or action is justified by
the available evidence.

---

# LIMEN

The boundary separating:

measurement

↓

interpretation

↓

decision

↓

action

LIMEN governs admissibility rather than computation.

---

# Evidence

Observable information supporting or contradicting a scientific
claim.

---

# Falsification

Experimental evidence demonstrating that a hypothesis or
implementation fails under the declared protocol.

---

# Equivalence

A result showing that a new implementation does not provide
measurable additional information relative to an existing one.

---

# Baseline

The frozen reference interval used for estimating candidate
identity.

---

# Evaluation Interval

The observation period during which identity continuity is
measured.

---

# Identity Degradation

Loss of continuity within the candidate identity network relative
to the frozen baseline.

---

# Provenance

Complete traceability of data, configuration, implementation and
outputs.

---

# Reproducibility

The ability to regenerate identical analytical results from the
same frozen inputs and configuration.

---

# Transferability

The ability of a methodological principle to remain valid across
independent domains after experimental validation.

Transferability must always be demonstrated experimentally.

---

# Framework

The complete theoretical architecture defining principles,
metrics, experiments and implementation boundaries.

---

# Engine

A software implementation of the Congruity Framework within a
specific application domain.

Example:

CGIE-3 is the geophysical engine of the Congruity Framework.

---

# Metric

A quantitative representation of a declared property within the
Framework.

Metrics must be defined in the Metric Registry before official
use.

---

# Experiment

A frozen protocol designed to test one or more hypotheses under
controlled and reproducible conditions.

---

# Scientific Outcome

An experiment may conclude with:

- supported;
- equivalent;
- inconclusive;
- non-discriminating;
- falsified.

All outcomes are scientifically valid.
